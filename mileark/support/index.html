Mileark Support

Answers to the questions that come up most. If yours isn't here, email nur.ahmed@outlook.com.

Trips aren't recording automatically

Automatic detection needs three things, all shown in the setup checklist on the Trips tab: Location set to Always, Precise Location turned on, and Motion & Fitness allowed. "While Using the App" is not enough — iOS will not wake the app for a drive you start with your phone in your pocket. Check these under Settings → Privacy & Security on your iPhone.

A trip started late or missed the first part of the drive

iOS wakes the app once you have moved a short distance, so recording begins slightly after you pull away. Mileark compensates by snapping the start back to where your previous trip ended and adding the missed distance. If a trip still looks short, open it and correct the mileage, or add a manual trip.

A trip didn't stop when I parked

Trips end after you have been parked for a few minutes — adjustable under Settings → "Stop after parked". Shorter settings end trips faster but can split one drive in two at a long light or drive-through; if that happens, open the trip and use Merge with previous trip.

A trip was classified wrong

Swipe any trip to reclassify it, or tap it and change the category. With "Learn from my corrections" enabled, classifying the same destination the same way twice teaches Mileark to do it for you next time. Rules it has learned are listed under Places and can be deleted.

The same place is sometimes business, sometimes personal

Save it under Places and turn on Ask me each time. Trips there won't be auto-classified; the trip notification will ask, and you can answer from the Lock Screen. Trips that are clearly part of a business run — arriving from or heading to a business place — are still classified automatically.

Which trips are actually deductible?

Mileark applies the current IRS standard mileage rates by trip date: business, medical, and charity trips each have their own rate, while commuting between home and a regular workplace is logged but not deductible. Mark a saved place as "Regular workplace" only if it is a workplace you commute to regularly — client sites, rental properties, and job sites should stay ordinary business places so their trips remain deductible. Mileark is a record-keeping tool, not tax advice; check your situation with a tax professional.

Preparing a report for my accountant

Go to Reports, choose a period, and export CSV or PDF. Leave "Deductible trips only" on to itemize business, medical, and charity trips while reducing personal driving to the mile totals tax forms ask for. Add a business purpose to each deductible trip — the IRS expects one — using a saved place's default purpose or the AI fill button.

Battery use

Between drives Mileark uses low-power location wakeups rather than continuous GPS, so background impact is small. Precise tracking runs only while you are actually driving.

Backing up my log

Settings → Export full backup writes a single file containing every trip, place, rule, and odometer reading. Save it to Files or iCloud Drive. Restore from backup merges it back without creating duplicates. Your log lives only on your device, so keeping a backup is worthwhile.
